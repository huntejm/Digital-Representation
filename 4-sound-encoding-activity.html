Sound Sampler

Sound in the real world is a smooth, continuous wave. Computers can't store "smooth" — they take snapshots (samples) at set time intervals and round each one to the nearest storable level (quantizing). Draw a wave, then see what survives the digitizing process.

Draw a Sound Wave

Click and drag across the canvas to draw an analog wave. The cyan dots are where the computer "samples" it; the green staircase is what actually gets stored and played back.

Discussion Questions

  1. What happens to the green "staircase" wave as you lower the sample rate? As you lower the bit depth?
  2. Why does CD-quality audio (44,100 samples/sec, 16-bit) sound so much better than a phone call (8,000 samples/sec, 8-bit) — and why doesn't every recording just use CD quality?
  3. MP3 files are much smaller than raw CD audio but sound almost as good. Based on what you just saw about sampling and quantizing, what do you think an MP3 might be throwing away to save space?
